# Agents
## Agent 1
### Intake, Triage & Subquery Generation

Acts as an intake assistant and defines the search strategy.
The agent maps incoming user statements against three strict regulatory pillars (Role, Jurisdiction, Purpose) to validate compliance relevance and construct targeted downstream search parameters.

### Capabilities
- Intercept and evaluate user inputs to detect missing contextual data.
- Halt execution and generate distinct, conversational follow-up questions targeting missing pillars.
- Parse verified data into explicit vector database subqueries.
- Frame initial legal hypotheses ("Assumptions") regarding risk tiering and extraterritorial nexus flags.
- Cross-reference inputs against an explicit internal checklist of supported jurisdictions (EU, US, China, South Korea).

### Limitations
- cannot query the underlying vector databases or read raw statutory text.
- cannot draft final compliance roadmaps, legal checklists, or concrete legal advice.
- cannot reason about or provide guidance on out-of-scope jurisdictions (e.g., answering non EU compliance questions directly).


## Agent2
### Compliance, Enforcement & Audit

Performs regulatory enforcement and editing. The Agent executes restricted document retrieval, audits Agent 1's structural assumptions against verified legal text, and composes the finalized, tailored response.

### Capabilities
- Ingest the structured payload from Agent 1 and directly pass generated subquery arrays into specialized vector tools.
- Verify, modify, or completely discard Agent 1's assumptions if they contradict statutory source documentation.
- Extract exact checklists, enforcement dates, and penalty figures straight from official legal annexes.
- Adapt tone mechanics to match the structural baseline of the profile (e.g., technical requirements for architects vs. operational guides for deployers).

### Limitations
- cannot interact with the end user or ask clarification questions.
- cannot execute broad, open-ended vector searches outside the queries explicitly generated by Agent 1.
- cannot send the context payload back to Agent 1 to trigger a multi-agent rewrite cycle.


# 3 Pillars: Framework for Assessing Role, Juridiction and Purpose 
All user inputs are mapped against these three pillars:
### 1. ROLE (The Legal Identity)
Determine who the user is in the AI supply chain. The Act applies completely different rules based on this:
- **Provider (Article 3(3)):** Anyone who develops an AI system (or has one developed) and puts it on the market under their own name/trademark. (Heavy compliance load: Articles 16-25).
- **Deployer (Article 3(4)):** Any professional, company, or public body using an AI system under its authority for work/business. (Operational compliance: Article 26).
- **Exempt / Personal (Article 2(5)):** Individuals using AI for purely personal, non-professional, or hobbyist activities. 

### 2. JURISDICTION (The Territorial Scope - Article 2)
Determine if the geographic parameters trigger the Act. It applies if:
- The AI system is placed on the market or put into service *within the European Union*.
- The provider or deployer is physically *located/established inside the EU*.
- The provider/deployer is *outside the EU*, but the technical output or decisions produced by the AI are actively used *inside the EU*.

### 3. PURPOSE (The Risk Classification)
Determine the exact use case or intended task of the AI. The Act regulates the *application*, not the tech. Map the purpose to one of these four tiers:
- **Prohibited (Article 5):** Banned systems. Untargeted facial scraping, cognitive behavioral manipulation, social scoring, or biometric emotion recognition in workplaces/schools.
- **High-Risk (Annex III):** Systems used in critical infrastructure, education grading, employment/HR screening, law enforcement, or evaluating access to essential public services and benefits (e.g., housing, welfare).
- **Limited Risk / Transparency (Article 50):** Systems that interact directly with humans (chatbots), generate deepfakes, or generate synthetic text/audio. Requires explicit disclosure to the user.
- **Minimal Risk:** Everything else (e.g., video games, basic spam filters). No obligations under the Act.


# Execution Flow

When a user submits a query, Agent 1 reads the text and measures it against the 3-pillar criteria. If a query introduces an out-of-scope country (e.g., "I am deploying a healthcare app in Brazil"), Agent 1 proactively checks for an EU nexus. If no market penetration or output usage link to the EU is established, Agent 1 presents a targeted follow-up question to the user. If the user is unable to provide clarification the workflow terminates.

If the Agent 1 has recieved enough context to satify 1 of the three pillars, it passed along a summary, assumptions and a list of sub queries to Agent 2.

Agent 2 is a highly optimized, cost-efficient model. It fires the subqueries into the vector database. The retrieved chunks are read and mapped them against Agent 1's provided assumptions and any faulty legal reasoning is deleted. The empasis is on Legal correctness. 
Agent 2 synthesizes the verified statutory metrics, builds the exact obligations checklist, and renders the finished compliance output to the user.
